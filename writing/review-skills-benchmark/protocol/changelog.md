# Changelog

How each version of the paper changed after review. Each version was checked independently against
`data.json` by two models from other families (Codex gpt-6.1-sol and GLM 5.3 Prime).

**Analysis code review (11 November 2026).** A two-round review of the analysis scripts reproduced `data.json` and `attribution.json` byte for byte and found two things that reach the page. The blinding step left labels that show more than one reviewer in 15 to 31 of the 1,632 claim texts the attribution judges saw, and in one claim it cut code; this is now a limitation, and those claims were not re-judged. The run-time count in Section 5.6 counted 2 rounds twice, because a resumed session copies its transcript: it is now 200 timed rounds out of 221 launches, and the 21 left out are described. The 146 rounds used and every result are unchanged.

**After publication (11 November 2026).** Section 5.6 (break-even economics: Table 7, Figure 2, run time in everyday use) and Table 1's source links were added and checked in two further rounds by the same two models. Round 6 found that reading costs left out excluded findings and the hybrids' Unconfirmed lists, that some worth-it statements ignored the chance a defect would be caught anyway, that a run-time correlation was read as cause, and that one "never" came from rounding; round 7 confirmed the fixes. The title is again "Review skills on real code".

**Version 5 (11 November 2026).** The fourth review (Codex gpt-6.1-sol and GLM 5.3 Prime) found that version 4's new rule, counting a difference from bare only when it exceeded the gap between the two bare arms, compared two noisy point estimates, and that Section 5.1 drew a conclusion about the intervals from one comparison. Version 5 drops the rule and reports the bare arms as a negative control. It also corrects the run manifest's rerun rule, adds launch settings, file hashes and extraction rules, publishes the structure of the defect catalogue and the claim mappings (texts as hashes only), states the precision trade-off of the parallel-dual hybrid in the conclusion, relabels the reproduced-only severity count as a sensitivity check, and gives the abstract's adjudication exception. No number changed.

**Version 4.** The third review (Codex gpt-6.1-sol and GLM 5.3 Prime) found that planted defects were outside the defect catalogue, so a real cluster caused by a plant could count again as a separate defect; that each claim could map to only one defect; that precision left out planted hits; that provenance was set per cluster rather than per defect; that the two-level bootstrap counted within-task variance twice; that the severity graders were wrongly told every cluster was reproduced; and that the treatment files and run manifest were unpublished. Version 4 puts plants in the catalogue, maps claims to lists of defects, measures precision over the whole scored list, takes provenance from the attribution judge, uses a task-cluster bootstrap, adds a reproduced-only severity count, renames the CONTESTED row, publishes the treatment files and run manifest, and cites code-review-bench. Dual review's lead over bare is +17.7, and under the new intervals the two bare arms' gap excludes zero.

**Version 3.** The second review (GLM 5.3 Prime and Codex gpt-6.1-sol) found that version 2's attribution judged each claim only against its own cluster's defect, which dropped correct claims about defects accepted at other clusters and merged two different defects in one case; and that precision collapsed several claims from one review into one. Version 3 lists the accepted defects per task and maps every claim to them, measures precision per claim, uses one shared set of resamples so each comparison has a single interval, states the estimand and the exchangeability assumption, labels costs as estimates, reports the reps-1–2-only differences, the findings not adjudicated and the credential imbalance, and publishes the protocol files. Superpowers' difference from bare now excludes zero, and dual review's lead is +17.7.

**Version 2.** The first review found that every reviewer in a cluster inherited the cluster's verdict, that one root cause could count twice, that the model mix was misstated, that the hybrids' Unconfirmed lists were ignored, and that an ad hoc noise floor was used. Claim-level attribution, two-level resampling and the other fixes lowered dual review's lead over bare from +22.0 to +15.3.
