What running it has taught me

Machines, services and public surfaces (the parts reachable from the internet) are kept separate. That makes a change easier to reason about, and a failure on one machine less likely to spread to the others.

One service spent weeks returning nothing because its configuration pointed at an IP address the machine no longer held. Everything was up and nothing errored, but the results were empty. The fix was to stop referring to that machine by IP address.

Another failure involved a workload that had to reach the internet through one specific network interface. Restarting things in the right order did not guarantee that it used that interface. The lesson was to bind the workload to the interface by name.
