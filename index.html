Recent work

Most of this year has gone into the plumbing underneath AI agents. terse is a proxy that sits in front of MCP tool servers and losslessly compresses what they return — typically 30 to 50 percent fewer tokens, under a rule that anything it cannot perfectly reconstruct gets forwarded untouched instead. Behind it is a knowledge graph on Postgres with a curated write path: an agent proposes a fact, a reviewer accepts or rejects it, and nothing lands unreviewed. Moving its embeddings to a sidecar took that service's production image from 2.14 GB to 442 MB.

The data side is spatial and statistical modeling where the answer has to hold up. Working with fifty thousand Michigan well records, the most useful result last quarter was a negative one: a promising prediction target turned out to be about 96 percent a restatement of well depth, so it was cut instead of shipped. The same instinct shows up in a fantasy-football auction board that returns null rather than a fabricated reference value, and in a news pipeline where the language model may only confirm or deny candidates a deterministic matcher already found — it is never allowed to invent one.

Under all of it is a ten-node rack running Proxmox, Caddy at the edge, redundant DNS, monitoring, and backups. Public routes come through Cloudflare Tunnel; nothing is port-forwarded. Before this — and still alongside it — the work was operational: CRM workflow state, event handling, and the reporting layer into Snowflake, BigQuery, and PowerBI. That is the part that taught me to care about what happened, what happens next, and whether anyone can trust the answer.
